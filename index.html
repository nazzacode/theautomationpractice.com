06 Your data

Your client data stays yours.

This is the real reason most firms stop before they start, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a reassuring noise.

The catch

Free tools can learn from what you upload

That's a genuine problem when the file is a client's accounts, a case bundle or a tenancy agreement. If someone in your firm has been told "don't put anything real into ChatGPT", they were right to be careful.

The fix

Paid and enterprise tiers don't

Training switched off, a data processing agreement in place, your work kept out of the training set. Setting that up properly is part of the audit — and it's what unlocks AI for the work you thought was off-limits.

Do we need anyone technical on our side?

No. That's the whole point. We work with firms whose idea of IT is the person who knows how to fix the printer. Everything we set up is meant to be used by the people already doing the job.

Is our team going to have to learn a lot?

Less than you'd think. These tools are powerful enough that a little training goes a long way — that's why half a day gets so much done. The single biggest win is a habit, not a skill: before any admin job, ask "can AI do this?"

What if we already use Microsoft Copilot?

Copilot sits nicely inside Outlook and Excel, but it's behind the best models available today. We'd generally point you at Claude or ChatGPT for the heavy lifting for now, and revisit as it improves. We'll tell you straight either way.

What does it cost?

The audit is a fixed price, discounted for early clients, and we'll tell you the number on the first call before you commit to anything. Ongoing work is quoted separately and only if you want it. We'd rather talk about where your time goes first.

What if there's nothing worth automating?

Then we'll say so, and you'll still leave the audit with your tools set up, your team trained and a written plan. In practice we've not yet walked into a firm with no repetitive work in it.
